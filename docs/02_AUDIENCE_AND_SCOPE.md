# Audience and Scope

## Core audience definition

Grow is designed primarily for people who are still forming or questioning their educational and career direction.

The core audience is defined by decision stage, not by a rigid age band.

## Primary audience

Students from Grade 8 through graduation.

Typical needs include:

1. Understanding personal strengths
2. Discovering interests that are not yet obvious
3. Choosing subjects or streams
4. Selecting college or university direction
5. Choosing between academic and skill based routes
6. Understanding possible careers connected to a degree
7. Avoiding choices made only from peer or family pressure
8. Comparing earning potential with actual fit

## Flexible age principle

Age should be captured but should not be the main eligibility rule.

A 15 year old student may urgently need subject direction.

A 22 year old graduate may still have no career clarity.

A 29 year old professional may have worked for several years without discovering a good fit.

The system should therefore separate audience tracks by life stage and problem type.

## Audience tracks

### Track A: School discovery

Typical stage: Grade 8 through Grade 10

Key questions:

1. What am I naturally drawn toward
2. What subjects or learning paths fit me
3. What should I explore before making a major stream decision
4. What abilities need development

### Track B: College direction

Typical stage: intermediate, A Levels, diploma, or equivalent

Key questions:

1. Which degree or skill route fits
2. Which fields should be tested before admission decisions
3. What are the tradeoffs between different paths
4. What is realistic given finances, grades, location, and family context

### Track C: University and graduation direction

Typical stage: undergraduate or recently graduated

Key questions:

1. What career roles fit my degree and my actual traits
2. Should I stay within my academic field
3. What skills are missing
4. What entry paths exist
5. What should I test before committing further

### Track D: Career reset

Typical stage: working adult or person returning to work

Key questions:

1. Why does the current field feel wrong
2. Which existing skills transfer elsewhere
3. Which alternative careers fit better
4. What income disruption is tolerable
5. How can a transition happen with controlled risk

## Initial geography

### Phase 1

Karachi

Reasons:

1. Easier operator access
2. Diverse education backgrounds
3. Broad career exposure
4. Large student population
5. Easier interview and pilot operations

### Phase 2

Sindh and Punjab

The product should account for different levels of access, language comfort, educational opportunity, family expectations, and local employment structure.

### Phase 3

Pakistan

National expansion should add regional language capability and local opportunity knowledge rather than simply increase participant count.

## Language

Participant facing primary language: Urdu

Future supported languages may include:

1. Sindhi
2. Punjabi
3. Saraiki
4. Pashto

The participant should be able to use voice messages when writing is difficult.

## Education range

The system should be understandable to a Grade 8 participant but still useful to a graduate or experienced worker.

This requires adaptive depth.

The same question should not be phrased identically for every education level.

## Inclusion principles

Grow should remain usable for participants who:

1. Have weak English
2. Have no laptop
3. Have only a smartphone
4. Come from government schools
5. Have interrupted education
6. Live outside major cities
7. Cannot pay the full fee
8. Have never taken a personality or career assessment
9. Are unsure how to describe their own strengths

## Exclusion and referral boundaries

Grow is not designed to diagnose mental health conditions, learning disabilities, or neurodevelopmental conditions.

If a conversation reveals a serious issue outside career guidance, the system should avoid pretending to solve it and should escalate or suggest appropriate human support.

## Parent role

For minors, parents or guardians may be involved in consent and practical decisions.

However, Grow should preserve the participant voice.

A parent can provide context, but the system should not silently replace the student preference with the parent preference.

## Audience quality principle

Grow should not optimise only for volume.

A smaller group of participants who complete assessment, experiments, and follow up is more valuable than a large list of passive contacts.

The acquisition system should therefore measure both entry volume and participant seriousness.
