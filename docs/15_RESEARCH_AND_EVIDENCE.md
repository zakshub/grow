# Research and Evidence Standards

## Purpose

Grow should not invent career advice from intuition alone.

Every durable rule, market claim, salary claim, assessment dimension, and recommendation pattern should have an evidence trail.

## Evidence classes

### Class A: Established external evidence

Examples:

1. Validated vocational interest frameworks
2. Peer reviewed personality research
3. Official labour force data
4. Official education and training data
5. Government qualification frameworks
6. Published occupational data

### Class B: Current market evidence

Examples:

1. Job postings
2. Employer demand
3. Salary reports
4. Freelance demand
5. Remote role availability
6. Training provider availability

This evidence changes quickly and must include a date.

### Class C: Grow field evidence

Examples:

1. Participant assessment patterns
2. Experiment outcomes
3. Human reviewer corrections
4. Follow up outcomes
5. Dropout behaviour
6. Regional differences

### Class D: Hypothesis

An idea that appears plausible but is not yet strong enough to guide high confidence decisions.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order when possible:

1. Official government or framework source
2. Primary research source
3. Recognised professional or educational body
4. High quality market data provider
5. Large job platform data
6. Expert interpretation
7. Community or anecdotal evidence

A lower source can still be useful, but confidence must reflect source quality.

## Career assessment research foundation

### O*NET Interest Profiler

Official source:

https://www.onetcenter.org/IP.html

O*NET uses the six RIASEC vocational interest areas:

1. Realistic
2. Investigative
3. Artistic
4. Social
5. Enterprising
6. Conventional

The framework is useful because it connects interests to occupations and has a long research history.

Grow should study it as a foundation, not blindly copy it.

O*NET also provides web services for career exploration integration:

https://services.onetcenter.org/ip

### Personality frameworks

16Personalities is useful as a familiar example of accessible personality communication, especially its use of trait spectrums rather than only a type label.

Official overview:

https://www.16personalities.com/articles/what-are-the-big-five-personality-traits

Official AI reference and framework boundary notes:

https://www.16personalities.com/for-ai

Grow should not copy proprietary questions or treat a type code as a career prescription.

The stronger design direction is dimensional personality evidence combined with vocational interests, strengths, values, and observed behaviour.

## Pakistan labour market evidence

### Pakistan Bureau of Statistics

Official labour force publications:

https://www.pbs.gov.pk/labour-force-publications/

Use for national and provincial employment context, occupational structure, labour force participation, unemployment, wage evidence, and related official statistics.

Important rule:

National averages are context, not a salary prediction for one career or one person.

### NAVTTC

Official training programme source:

https://navttc.gov.pk/programs/

Use for vocational trades, skills programmes, qualification pathways, and public training opportunities.

### Higher Education Commission

Use official HEC sources for recognised higher education pathways, institutions, qualification context, and related policy evidence.

Career portal example:

https://careers.hec.gov.pk/

Do not infer the entire graduate labour market from HEC vacancies.

## Market evidence model

Every career market record should store:

1. Career identifier
2. Geography
3. Evidence type
4. Source
5. Observation date
6. Value or finding
7. Confidence
8. Notes
9. Expiry or refresh date

## Salary evidence

Never show a salary as a guaranteed number.

Prefer ranges.

Separate:

1. Entry level
2. Early career
3. Experienced
4. Local employment
5. Remote employment
6. Freelance income

Salary evidence should include geography and date.

## Opportunity evidence

A career can have strong personality fit and weak local opportunity.

Grow should show both.

Suggested opportunity dimensions:

1. Local demand
2. Remote demand
3. Freelance demand
4. Training availability
5. Qualification barriers
6. Entry competition
7. Geographic concentration
8. Automation exposure
9. International mobility

## Evidence confidence

Suggested levels:

### High

Multiple strong and recent sources agree.

### Moderate

Reasonable evidence exists but is incomplete or geographically weak.

### Low

Evidence is sparse, old, indirect, or conflicting.

### Unknown

No responsible conclusion can currently be made.

Unknown is a valid result.

## Freshness rules

Different evidence requires different refresh cycles.

Examples:

1. Personality framework research can refresh slowly
2. Education programme availability should refresh at least annually or when admissions change
3. Salary and hiring demand should refresh frequently
4. WhatsApp platform rules should be checked before implementation changes
5. Participant learning should be reviewed continuously

## Pakistan adaptation rule

Do not import a foreign career framework and assume direct equivalence.

Validate:

1. Language
2. Cultural interpretation
3. Education structure
4. Career availability
5. Family decision patterns
6. Gender access constraints
7. Urban and rural differences
8. Device and internet access

## Research log format

Every substantial research entry should record:

1. Question
2. Source
3. Date accessed
4. Geography
5. Main finding
6. Limitations
7. Product implication
8. Confidence
9. Whether it changes an existing rule

## Knowledge promotion

External evidence plus Grow field evidence can produce a hypothesis.

A hypothesis becomes a promoted rule only after review.

Do not silently rewrite the brain from one article, one participant, one expert, or one trend.

## Evidence rule

If Grow does not know, it should say unknown and create a research task.

False certainty is more damaging than an explicit gap.
