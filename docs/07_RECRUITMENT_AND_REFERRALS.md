# Recruitment and Referrals

## Goal

Build a repeatable acquisition system that finds serious participants without depending on random social media reach.

The initial objective is not maximum volume.

It is to discover which sources consistently produce participants who actually complete the process and benefit from it.

## Initial acquisition sources

1. Old students
2. Friends and family networks
3. Village and community networks
4. School and college contacts
5. Teachers
6. University students
7. WhatsApp groups
8. Existing participants
9. Community organisers
10. Local youth groups
11. Social media
12. Direct outreach where appropriate

## First pilot principle

Start with warm networks before paid growth.

Reasons:

1. Easier trust
2. Faster feedback
3. Better interview access
4. Lower acquisition cost
5. Stronger referral loops
6. Easier detection of poor messaging

## Outreach expectation

Do not assume a high response rate.

If 100 people are approached, only a fraction may respond and a smaller fraction may complete the process.

The system should treat this as funnel behaviour, not operator failure.

## Outreach workflow

### Step 1

Build a source list.

Suggested fields:

1. Contact name
2. Source
3. Referrer
4. Education level
5. Approximate age band
6. City or area
7. Contact status
8. Response status
9. Assessment status
10. Final outcome

### Step 2

Send a clear invitation explaining the value.

The invitation should focus on career confusion and self discovery, not on free access.

### Step 3

Move interested people into the official WhatsApp flow.

### Step 4

Track completion quality by source.

## Referral system

After a participant receives meaningful value, ask for referrals.

Suggested request:

Refer people who genuinely do not understand what direction suits them and are willing to complete the discovery process.

Avoid asking for random contact lists.

The referred person should initiate or consent to contact where possible.

## Referral attribution

Track:

1. Referrer identifier
2. Acquisition source
3. Date
4. Participant completion
5. Recommendation completion
6. Follow up outcome

This enables source quality analysis.

## Source quality

Do not judge a source only by number of leads.

Useful measures include:

1. Response rate
2. Assessment completion rate
3. Practical experiment completion
4. Recommendation completion
5. Follow up response
6. Referral quality
7. Payment completion where relevant
8. Long term participant outcome

## Geographic learning

Track source performance by location.

Over time Grow should learn:

1. Which channels work in Karachi
2. Which channels work in smaller cities
3. Which community relationships build trust
4. Where guardian involvement is more important
5. Which languages increase completion
6. Which sources produce serious participants rather than curiosity traffic

## Girls and women

Recruitment design should recognise safety and trust realities.

Potential improvements include:

1. Female outreach partners where appropriate
2. Clear privacy language
3. Guardian involvement for minors
4. Remote participation
5. No unnecessary photo requirement
6. Predictable communication times
7. Clear identity and purpose of the programme

Do not treat these as universal assumptions. Validate them by context.

## Outreach data boundary

The acquisition list contains personal information and must not be stored in the public GitHub repository.

GitHub contains only schemas, logic, templates, and anonymised aggregate learning.

## Initial target

For the first field test, prioritise enough outreach to produce a small but meaningful group of completed participants.

A useful first target is not 1,000 leads.

A more useful target is a cohort large enough to reveal whether:

1. People understand the offer
2. They complete the assessment
3. The assessment produces useful differentiation
4. Practical experiments improve recommendations
5. Participants consider the result useful

## Growth rule

Do not scale acquisition faster than recommendation quality.

If the system cannot yet guide 20 participants well, acquiring 2,000 participants only scales confusion.
